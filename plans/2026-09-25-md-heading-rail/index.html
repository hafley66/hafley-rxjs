▾ 1. Heading rail prototype

One document column, one left rail. This page mirrors the md panel's real DOM shape — .mdview-content › .mdview-sec › .mdview-head + .mdview-body — and every variant is a pure CSS customization of those class names. Switching variants and reading the variable sheet works with radio inputs and :has() alone; the page carries no script.

  • Watch the rail change colour as each heading arrives.
  • Section 1.3 is folded: its rail answers the folded-section question.
  • Section 1.2 holds a numbered list, the case where list guides crowd the rail.
▾ 1.1 One rail, many levels

Headings of every level sit flush on the same rail edge — no stacked per-level guides, no per-level indent of the rail.

  • The heading box keeps square corners at the rail and rounds away from it.
  • The fold toggle is a small circle centred on the rail, mid-height of the box.
▾ 1.1.1 Blend mechanics

Each section paints its own rail segment. --prev is the parent level's colour, --cur this level's; the segment ramps between the two.

  • Inter-section gaps belong to the parent, so sibling boundaries never tear.
▾ 1.2 Numbered steps

A numbered list — the shape the list guides and the rail have to share space with.

  1. Pick a variant above; the sheet on the right lists its variables.
  2. Read the rail's colour ramp against the list's own guide.
  3. Check that markers and guides never cross the rail.
▸ 1.3 Folded reference

v3 lab — collision blend

Rule 3 readings: where a rail meets a heading, the stroke stains the box's fill. Every swatch is the same scene — a level-3 heading box with the outermost rail under its left border and its own rail crossing two indents in — driven by the same knobs on .rail-lab in rail-lab.css: --stroke, --h-1…6, --spill-size, --spill-strength. Each shows light and dark paper. The variants take two of these readings: v3-stain → radial stain (+ ancestor wash), v3-diffuse → diffuse glow.
